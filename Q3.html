The Founding Father

Tim Berners-Lee: Inventor Of The World Wide Web

The Article

Berners-Lee’s original research concerns physics because, during his early life, Berners-Lee majored in physics—a bridge between his interests in theoretical mathematics and practical electronics and engineering—at Queen’s College, Oxford University (1972), thereby providing his foundational scientific training before turning to software engineering, where he eventually invented the World Wide Web while working at CERN (the European Organization for Nuclear Research) in Switzerland.

Compared to that, the current users—or “users,” I should say—do not have to concern themselves with physics, as the World Wide Web has allowed many inventors and researchers to develop further into the internet, where the majority now can just quickly search and use the popular search engine, Google, for quick answers and simple inquiries seamlessly.

(Tim Berners-Lee)
